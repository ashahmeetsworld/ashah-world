Give before you ask. Here's what that looks like for me right now. ͏ ͏ ͏ ͏ ͏ ͏

A few weeks ago I had an offer ready to go. A name, a deck, a landing page.

I put it on the shelf.

It wasn't yet at the standard I want everything with my name on it to meet. Watching Iman Gadzhi hold his own work to a standard of excellence made me look at mine more honestly. When I did, I could see the offer was built on what I assumed people needed, not what they'd told me.

Going to them first

So I'm not waiting for the right people to find me. I'm going out to them, with something in hand. That's the gift on the pedestal up top: give before you ask.

Right now my job isn't to sell. It's to reach out, listen, and be a sponge.

What I listen for

•The gap between their vision and their reality.
•What's frustrating them this week, in their own words.
•The bottleneck that keeps showing up, not just one bad day.
•What they value, and what they'd protect if everything else had to go.
•What they've already tried, and why it didn't stick.

The last one tells me more than the rest put together. If someone's tried three things and none worked, the fourth thing I offer had better be different in kind, not just in name.

Why it matters to me

Contribution is one of my core values. The version of me I respect most is the one who leaves people with more than they had. When I was working out what A·M·W is for, I wrote this down:

“To never be a gate-keeper, and to unlock possibilities for other people.”

So for now, the doors are open.

If you're building something too

Try this before your next offer:

1.Talk to five people you'd love to serve.
2.Ask what they've already tried.
3.Write down their words, not your summary.
4.Give each of them something useful, with nothing attached.

Then look at your offer again. You'll know what to change.
